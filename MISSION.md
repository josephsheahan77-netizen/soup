# Local Bowl: why it exists

> **Some days you just want a good cup of soup.
> Some days you just need to dance.
> Here's the place to find out where.**

## Joy, and being generous

The point is joy: the small, real kind. You wanted chicken noodle, nobody has it today, but
there's tomato bisque down the street, and that's pretty good too. You got close to what you wanted
and you're happy. The app should feel like that every time you open it.

So we stay **generous, not clever**. Nothing in the app tries to trick, upsell, or steer anyone.
When someone asks for a soup, they get the honest answer, and money never changes that answer.

## Who it's for

The person who works Monday through Friday and wants to know where lunch is today without
walking the whole town. The person who's had tomato basil three days running and wants French
onion. The person who wants to see a local band tonight without digging through ten Facebook pages.

Built for small towns and regions of roughly 10,000 to 100,000 people: Hood River, Jackson Hole,
Boise, Minocqua. Places big apps don't bother with.

## What it is

- **Soup:** what's in the soup pot today, at every lunch spot in town.
- **Music and events:** what's playing tonight. *(Coming next.)*

That's all. Just information: what, where, and today.

## What it is not

- Not a review site. No ratings, no comments, no complaints.
- Not a menu site. Soup, specials, a map link, and that's it.
- Not owned by social media. No Facebook or Instagram needed to post or to look.

## How it works

- **Everyone shares.** Restaurants, venues, bands, staff and customers all post. The business's
  own post wins over anyone else's. Others confirm with a check.
- **Fair by design.** The list is in a random order that reshuffles every day, then alphabetical.
  Nobody can pay for a better spot.
- **Each town sees its own town.** Someone in Astoria never sees Hood River soups.

## How it stays alive

- Free to use, free to be listed.
- Paid only by optional extras for businesses (never position) and one clearly marked local
  sponsor per town.
- Run small and sustainably: cheap to host, simple to understand, written down so someone else
  can take it over.
- If it's ever sold, it's sold for what it earns, to someone who keeps these rules.
